Most organizations already use more AI than they realize. Some of it was chosen deliberately: a chatbot on the website, a forecasting model in finance. Much more arrives quietly, as features inside productivity suites, CRM platforms, design tools and analytics products. Before you can govern any of it, you need to know it is there.

That is why an AI inventory is usually the most useful first step in an AI governance program. It turns a vague concern ("are we using AI responsibly?") into a list of specific systems, each with a purpose, an owner, and data you can assess.

## What belongs in an AI inventory

Keep the first version simple. For each AI system or AI-enabled feature, record:

- **What it is**: the product, model or feature, and whether it was built in-house, bought, or switched on inside an existing platform.
- **What it is used for**: the business process it supports and the decisions it influences.
- **Who owns it**: a named person accountable for its use, not just the team that installed it.
- **What data it uses**: especially personal, confidential or regulated information, in prompts, training or outputs.
- **Who is affected**: employees, customers or the public, and whether outputs reach them directly.

## How to find what's in use

Combine a few sources rather than relying on a survey alone. Procurement and contract records show vendors with AI features. IT and security teams can see which tools are accessed. Short conversations with business units surface the uses no system records. Release notes from your major platforms show AI features that have arrived through updates.

## From inventory to oversight

Once the inventory exists, it becomes the backbone of everything else. Risk tiering assigns each entry a level of scrutiny. Policies say what is allowed at each level. Review workflows route new entries to the right people. Monitoring keeps entries current.

### Keep it alive

An inventory that is accurate for one month and then forgotten does little good. Build a light routine: an intake form for new AI requests, a check during procurement, and a periodic review with system owners.
