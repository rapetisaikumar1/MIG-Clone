Adobe Experience Manager (AEM) helps businesses create, manage and optimize digital experiences across channels from one platform. This article covers the main features and benefits of AEM and how they help teams work more efficiently and deliver better digital experiences.

## What is Adobe Experience Manager?

Adobe Experience Manager is Adobe's content management solution. It combines a content management system (CMS), AEM Sites, with digital asset management (DAM), AEM Assets, so teams can create, manage and deliver content and assets for websites, apps and other channels from one platform. Most new implementations run as AEM as a Cloud Service, which Adobe hosts and keeps up to date.

## Key features of Adobe Experience Manager

### Content management (AEM Sites)

AEM Sites lets authors build and update pages from reusable components and templates in visual editors, without writing code. Sites built with Edge Delivery Services can also be authored in familiar documents, such as Microsoft Word or Google Docs, and are designed for fast page delivery. Either way, marketing teams can publish changes quickly while developers keep control of design and structure.

### Digital asset management (AEM Assets)

AEM Assets is a central library for images, videos, documents and other digital assets. Metadata, search, version history and automatically generated renditions make assets easy to find and reuse, and Dynamic Media delivers images and video in the right size and format for each device. A single source of approved assets keeps brands consistent across channels.

### Personalization and targeting

AEM supports personalized content based on audience, behavior and context. Experience Fragments, groups of content and layout that can be reused, can be sent to Adobe Target as offers for testing and personalization. When Target also uses audiences from Adobe Real-Time CDP, the same content can be tailored to each customer segment.

### Multi-channel delivery

Content Fragments store structured content, such as product descriptions or FAQs, separately from presentation. They can be delivered through AEM's GraphQL and REST APIs to mobile apps, single-page applications, in-store screens and other devices, as well as to web pages. Content is written once and reused wherever it is needed, so the experience stays consistent across platforms.

### Integration with other Adobe applications

AEM connects with other Adobe applications, such as Adobe Analytics, Adobe Target, Adobe Campaign and Adobe Experience Platform, and with Adobe Workfront for content planning and review. This gives teams a connected set of tools for data-driven marketing and customer engagement.

## Benefits of Adobe Experience Manager

### Improved efficiency

Managing content and assets in one platform, with reusable components, templates and fragments, reduces the time and effort needed to create, manage and deliver content. Teams stop rebuilding the same content for each channel.

### Enhanced collaboration

Workflows, review and approval steps, and annotations let authors, designers, reviewers and legal teams contribute to content in one place. This improves communication and alignment and shortens review cycles.

### Better user experience

Personalization, consistent branding and fast page delivery help businesses give audiences relevant, engaging content, which supports satisfaction and loyalty.

### Scalability

AEM as a Cloud Service scales with demand, and Multi Site Manager and language copies let organizations run many sites, brands and languages from shared content. Digital experiences can grow as the business does.

### Data-driven insights

Integration with Adobe Analytics and other Adobe applications shows how content performs and how users behave. Teams can then improve content based on evidence rather than opinion.
